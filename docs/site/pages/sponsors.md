# One person builds this. Sponsorship keeps it independent.

VayuPress is a single signed binary that replaces a website stack, a mail provider and a chat service — website & blog, VayuMail, VayuTalk, bot protection, analytics, PGP, an API, an MCP server and one-click Tor, self-hosted on hardware you control. No tracking, no phone-home, no venture capital, no plan to ever charge for the core.

That independence is the point — and it is also the funding model's weakness. There is no company behind this. Sponsorship is how the work continues at the pace it has: security audits answered with fixes, contributor PRs reviewed within a day, and signed releases shipped continuously.

[Sponsor on GitHub](https://github.com/sponsors/johalputt) · [Read the source first](https://github.com/johalputt/VayuPress)

## What sponsorship funds

- **Full-time development.** The goal is $5,000/month — the line where this stops competing with paid work and becomes the work. That is the whole reason the number exists: it is not a valuation, a revenue target, or a price anyone owes.
- **Security as a habit, not an event.** Recent months: a full audit remediated finding-by-finding, mail account recovery designed and adversarially tested, bot protection rebuilt to never lock out a human.
- **Infrastructure that stays yours.** Signed binaries, cosign verification, reproducible releases, live test servers — the unglamorous costs of software people trust with their mail.

## The honest part

Sponsoring does not buy priority features, private support or a say in the roadmap — the ADR process decides that, in public. What it buys is continuation: the same person who answered the last security report being around to answer the next one.

And it does not buy the licence.

VayuPress is Apache-2.0, permanently and unconditionally. That is settled and is not tied to the $5,000 figure above, or to any figure. **If this goal is never reached, nothing changes** — the project stays free, stays Apache-2.0, and stays maintained part-time. It does not get sold, relicensed or made source-available.

That ordering is the point. A project that would change its terms under financial pressure has not decided anything — it has only decided what it will do while things are going well. [Read the pledge and what backs it](../../LICENSING.md).

If money is not on the table, the other things that genuinely help: run it, report what breaks, and tell one person who is tired of renting their website from a platform.
